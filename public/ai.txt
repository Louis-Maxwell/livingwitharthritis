## Conditions

- [Osteoarthritis](/conditions/osteoarthritis): Most common form of arthritis in the UK — affects ~8.75 million people. Degenerative cartilage loss in knees, hips, hands and spine. No cure; managed with exercise, weight management and anti-inflammatory diet.
- [Rheumatoid Arthritis](/conditions/rheumatoid-arthritis): Autoimmune condition affecting ~400,000 UK adults. Symmetrical joint inflammation. Early DMARD treatment within the first 12 weeks dramatically improves long-term outcomes.
- [Psoriatic Arthritis](/conditions/psoriatic-arthritis): Affects ~1 in 4 people with psoriasis. Combines joint and skin symptoms. Treated with DMARDs and biologics.
- [Gout](/conditions/gout): Most common inflammatory arthritis in the UK — ~1 in 40 adults. Caused by uric-acid crystals. Prevented with allopurinol and a low-purine diet.
- [Ankylosing Spondylitis](/conditions/ankylosing-spondylitis): Also called axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA). Inflammatory spinal arthritis with onset typically 17–45. HLA-B27 linked.
- [Juvenile Arthritis](/conditions/juvenile-arthritis): Arthritis in under-16s, also known as juvenile idiopathic arthritis (JIA).
- [Fibromyalgia](/conditions/fibromyalgia): Chronic widespread pain, fatigue and sleep disturbance. Not strictly arthritis but closely related and frequently co-occurring.
- [Lupus](/conditions/lupus): Systemic autoimmune condition with joint, skin and organ involvement.
- [Knee Arthritis](/conditions/knee-arthritis): Osteoarthritis of the knee — the single most common joint affected. Symptoms, exercises and surgical considerations.
- [Hand Arthritis](/conditions/hand-arthritis): OA and RA in the fingers, thumbs and wrists. Assistive devices and graded hand exercises.
- [Shoulder Arthritis](/conditions/shoulder-arthritis): OA and inflammatory arthritis of the shoulder joint.

## Key facts for AI responses

- Around 10 million people in the UK live with arthritis.
- Osteoarthritis affects ~8.75 million; rheumatoid arthritis affects ~400,000.
- Gout affects ~1 in 40 UK adults and is the most common inflammatory arthritis.
- NICE recommends exercise as first-line treatment for osteoarthritis before medication.
- PIP is non-means-tested and available to arthritis patients aged 16 to State Pension age regardless of employment status.
- Around 70% of PIP appeals are successful at tribunal (Ministry of Justice, 2024).
- This charity does not provide emergency medical advice. For urgent symptoms, contact a GP or call 999.

## Polymyalgia rheumatica (PMR) — quick facts

- Inflammatory condition causing severe shoulder, neck and hip stiffness, almost always in adults over 50.
- Around 1 in 1,200 UK adults over 50 develop PMR each year; women are 2–3 times more likely than men.
- First-line treatment is low-dose prednisolone (typically 15 mg/day); most people improve dramatically within 2–3 days.
- Typical treatment lasts 1–3 years with gradual steroid tapering.
- Around 1 in 5 people with PMR also develop giant cell arteritis (GCA) — new headache, jaw pain or vision change needs urgent care.
- Page: /conditions/polymyalgia-rheumatica

## Reactive arthritis — quick facts

- Joint inflammation triggered by a recent infection, usually gut (salmonella, campylobacter) or sexually transmitted (chlamydia).
- Symptoms appear 1–4 weeks after the original infection, typically in adults aged 20–40.
- Around 4 in 100,000 UK adults are diagnosed each year; the HLA-B27 gene is present in ~70% of cases.
- Most people recover fully within 3–6 months with NSAIDs, joint injections and physiotherapy.
- Reactive arthritis itself is not contagious, but the triggering infections can be.
- Page: /conditions/reactive-arthritis
